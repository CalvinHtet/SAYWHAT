# How Quest Board talks

Cal, 2026-11-05: "make sure it is professional and not corny or too obvious that it is AI."

Every line in the game (item and upgrade descriptions, buttons, toasts, help, dialogue) follows this page. LEAD owns it.
ROOMS writes room and upgrade lines to it, QA flags lines that break it.

## The rule in one line

Say what the thing is, in a detail only someone who has seen it would know. Then stop.

## Descriptions (items, upgrades, rooms, relics)

- One sentence, two at most. Under about 90 characters for upgrades and items.
- Lead with a concrete, physical detail: a material, a colour, a sound, where it sits, who uses it.
  Good: "Walnut counter, brass rail, six stools." Bad: "The perfect spot to unwind in style."
- No punchline on the end. A line can be dry or warm, but it is not a setup for a joke. At most one light line per
  screen, and only when it is actually funny.
- Say what it does in game terms only where the player needs it ("Missions pay 5% more coins"). That goes in the buff
  text, not the description.
- No selling: no "perfect", "ultimate", "stylish", "elevate", "next level", "game-changer", "must-have", "epic",
  "legendary" (unless it is the rarity), "boss", "vibes", "level up your".

## Dialogue

- People talk in short, plain sentences with contractions. They do not explain the plot to each other.
- Each character sounds like themselves:
  - **Mara** (chef): practical, proud of her food, a bit blunt. Talks about tables, orders, ingredients.
  - **Desmond Hale** (investor): measured, old money, notices details, never names a figure.
  - **Priya Okafor** (investor): quick, analytical, talks in foot traffic and leases, never names a figure.
  - **Rafe Calloway** (travelling adventurer): unhurried, specific places and drinks, understates danger.
  - **Nova Lin** (fashion vlogger): fast, current, but not a parody. No "slay", no "OMG", no emoji.
  - **Theo** (theater crew): quiet, competent, dry.
  - **Sable** (scout): terse. Reports what she saw.
- One line per screen. Let a scene end on something specific, not a summary or a moral.
- No one says the player is amazing. Praise comes as a detail ("Table six sent their compliments").

## Buttons, toasts and help

- Buttons are verbs: "Open it", "Send 2 people", "Collect". Not "Let's go!".
- Toasts say what happened: "Relic found. Unbroken I: +150 coins". Not "Woohoo!".
- Help explains in plain words, second person, present tense. One idea per sentence.

## Tells that read as written by AI (cut them)

- Exclamation marks. Use none outside a character's actual shout.
- "Not just X, it's Y", "X meets Y", "Whether you're X or Y".
- Lists of three adjectives ("sleek, modern and stylish").
- Em dashes. Use a full stop, a colon or a comma.
- Ending with a quip: "Productivity not guaranteed", "Obviously.", "Your back says thanks", "now decorative",
  "big feelings", "It probably works", "Don't tell anyone", "print money".
- Talking to the item or the player in a wink: "Someone has to water it", "You know you want it".
- Puns in names or lines.
- Words: delve, embark, journey, realm, unleash, elevate, seamless, curated, vibrant, testament, tapestry, bustling,
  cozy nook, oasis, haven, whimsical, sleek (once a screen at most).
- Emoji, ALL CAPS for emphasis, "!!".
